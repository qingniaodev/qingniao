On launch day, I installed analytics (Cloudflare Web Analytics) on my site. The next morning, I opened the dashboard — the first time I'd looked at my own traffic data.

This post records what I saw, and what it taught me.

## What the data looks like

Since launch (August 16 to the morning of the 17th), my site got **84 page loads**, with obvious bot traffic filtered out. Broken down by day: 71 on launch day, 13 on the second morning.

By page: the homepage took 57 of those, and the rest were spread across blog posts, tool pages, and the about page. By region, the most records came from Singapore (54), then the US (21) and Hong Kong (6). By device: mostly macOS, then iOS and Windows.

One source record stood out: **1 visit came from an external search engine.** The rest were either typed directly or came from internal navigation.

## What I actually saw

If you only look at "84", it's easy to conclude "not bad". But taken apart, I told myself: **don't get excited too early.**

First, most of the traffic came from launch day itself — the day I was building, deploying, testing, and fixing the www redirect. The vast majority of those visits were my own verification requests, not readers.

Second, the 54 records from Singapore have no clear explanation. I've done no promotion there. It could be some kind of monitoring traffic or prefetching — I can't be sure. **Data I can't explain isn't data I should tell stories with.**

Third, what actually caught my attention was that **1 visit from a search engine.** It means that with zero promotion, search engines have already started indexing my pages, and someone actually found them. It's the first tiny return on the "content before promotion" route.

## Three things I learned

1. **Having data isn't the same as reading it.** 84 is a number, but taken apart: mostly testing, some unexplained, very little actual readers. People who can read data look at the structure; people who can't look at the total.
2. **First-day numbers don't indicate any trend.** It's just the trace of "I clicked around a lot while building the site". Real judgment needs weeks or months of data.
3. **The point of analytics isn't the numbers, it's direction.** I didn't install analytics to show off traffic. I installed it to know: who comes, where from, what they look at. That's how I know whether my content is working.

## Closing

The first look at my own data brought no surprise and no disappointment. It's just a beginning — of those 84 page loads, most were my own footsteps. But one came from a search engine, and a few more came from people I don't know.

That's good. Two days after launch, my site already has visitors I can't account for. What comes next isn't staring at numbers — it's to keep writing and keep building, so those "1"s slowly become "10"s and "100"s, each one a real person reading.

Data is a mirror, not a report card. A mirror shows where you are; a report card is earned.
